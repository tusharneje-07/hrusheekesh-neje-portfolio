01 Overview

What is Biddano?

Biddano is India's largest B2B healthcare marketplace. It connects manufacturers, distributors, and retailers across the full supply chain - from factory floor to pharmacy shelf. With 50,000+ SKUs across pharmaceuticals, medical devices, OTC, and surgical supplies, the platform enables bulk procurement with verified suppliers, transparent pricing, and reliable logistics.

I designed the complete digital experience from scratch: user research, information architecture, flow design, interaction patterns, and high-fidelity UI across all core pages.

02 The Problem

A broken supply chain, digitized

India's healthcare supply chain is fragmented and opaque. Procurement often happens over calls, WhatsApp threads, and manual paper trails - creating trust issues, poor visibility, and daily friction.

Before - Pain Points
  • No unified platform to browse verified healthcare suppliers at scale
  • Procurement done via phone calls, WhatsApp, and paper trails
  • No transparent pricing or MOQ information upfront
  • Trust deficit: fake suppliers, expired stock, low accountability
  • No order tracking or fulfillment visibility for buyers
  • Seller onboarding was tedious and manual
  • Small pharmacies lacked access to competitive wholesale pricing
  • No analytics or demand forecasting layer
After - Design Decisions
  • Searchable marketplace with verified seller badges and reviews
  • Streamlined RFQ and order flow replacing informal channels
  • Price-per-unit, MOQ, and bulk discounts shown on listing cards
  • Seller verification UI with document status indicators
  • Real-time order tracking with milestone-based progress
  • Self-serve seller onboarding in under 10 steps
  • Buyer dashboard with personalized catalog and pricing
  • Analytics dashboard for sellers to track demand signals
03 Research & Discovery

Listening before designing

Structured interviews, contextual observation, surveys, and competitive analysis helped uncover the real barriers behind adoption in healthcare procurement workflows.

Research Methods

  • In-depth interviews with pharmacists, distributors, and manufacturers across Tier 1-3 cities
  • Contextual observation by shadowing pharmacists during ordering
  • Survey + desk research across India's pharma supply market
  • Competitive benchmarking: Pharmarack, PharmEasy B2B, Medikabazaar, Udaan Health

Key Insights

  • Trust is the #1 barrier when buyers consider unknown vendors
  • Price discovery is painful; many called 4-6 distributors daily
  • Catalog chaos from inconsistent product naming and spelling variants
  • Speed is critical; long catalog searches break repeat ordering behavior
04 Empathy Mapping

Getting inside the user's head

The primary persona was the pharmacy retailer. Their behavior and anxiety shaped trust architecture, reorder shortcuts, and visibility choices across the product.

Thinks & Feels

  • "I do not trust unknown distributors - what if medicine is fake?"
  • "I wish I could compare prices without calling everyone manually."
  • "I need better deals but I do not have time to negotiate."
  • Anxiety around expiry stock and returns

Says & Does

  • "Send me the rate list on WhatsApp."
  • Reorders the same brands from known distributors each week
  • Tracks inventory in register books or basic spreadsheets
  • "My distributor gives me credit. I will not switch easily."

Sees

  • Competitors adopting newer apps and improving margins
  • WhatsApp dominating B2B commerce in local networks
  • Patients demanding faster availability and broader variety
  • Large distributors providing richer ordering details

Pain Points & Gains

  • Pain: No centralized way to discover competitive pricing
  • Pain: Stock-outs caused by unreliable supply visibility
  • Gain: One-click reorder for regular products
  • Gain: Reliable delivery with digital proof and tracking
05 User Personas

Three archetypes, one platform

The core challenge was balancing three different procurement contexts without making the platform heavy for any one user group.

Retail Pharmacist

Price-sensitive daily restocker

Goals: Fast price comparison, maintain supplier credit relationships, reduce manual inventory effort.

Pain points: Calls 5+ distributors daily, cannot verify credentials online, fears fake products.

Medical Store Owner

Margin and continuity focused buyer

Goals: Improve margins, find backup suppliers, reduce dependence on one distributor.

Pain points: Frequent stock-outs, poor inventory visibility, inconsistent credit terms.

Ayurvedic Pharmacist

Quality and authenticity focused buyer

Goals: Source authentic herbal medicines, build customer trust, maintain stable inventory.

Pain points: Hard supplier verification, variable batch quality, limited digital supplier systems.

11 Design Principles

What guided every decision

The final product direction stayed anchored to three principles that consistently resolved trade-offs.

Trust by Design

Verified seller badges, GST transparency, drug license validation, and clear return policy visibility were designed to earn confidence before first purchase.

Speed is the Feature

Smart search, one-click reorder, saved carts, and pre-filled checkout fields shortened the path from intent to order.

Clarity over Completeness

Surfaced the most relevant decision data first: price, availability, and delivery ETA, instead of overwhelming spec-heavy views.

13 Reflections & Learnings

What I learned designing Biddano

01
Domain expertise unlocks design quality
Healthcare procurement UX needs regulatory grounding: Schedule H constraints, GSTIN requirements, and drug license validation.
02
Trust is a design problem
Trust improved through structure, not slogans: verification in context, transparent pricing, and visible return policy at checkout.
03
Multi-sided marketplaces require balance
Buyer-facing decisions always impact seller operations. Designing both sides in parallel prevented one-sided optimizations.
04
B2B users expect B2C-level polish
Business users compare experiences with top consumer apps daily. Polished UX is essential for adoption, not optional.
